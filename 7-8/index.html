Bolívar and Washington

A gift from Washington's family. In 1825, George Washington Parke Custis, Washington's adopted grandson, sent Bolívar a gold medallion with Washington's portrait and a lock of his hair, carried by the Marquis de Lafayette. They called Bolívar the “Washington of the South.” Bolívar received it in 1826 and treasured it. He admired Washington as the liberator of North America.Sources: Bolívar's letters to Lafayette (1826); Mount Vernon; Banco Central de Venezuela collection. The real medallion is kept by the Central Bank of Venezuela. The photo is shown for educational use; read more (in Spanish) at Monedas de Venezuela.

Timeline: Venezuela's story

1498 to 1806
Indigenous peoples (Caribs, Arawaks, Timoto-Cuicas). Columbus reaches the coast (1498); "Little Venice" (1499); Caracas founded (1567); Captaincy General (1777); Miranda's expedition (1806).
1810 to 1821
Independence war led by Simón Bolívar. Battle of Carabobo, 1821. Gran Colombia is formed.
1854
President José Gregorio Monagas signs the law abolishing slavery.
1958
Dictator Marcos Pérez Jiménez falls (January). Democracy and free elections begin.
1958 to 1983
Golden age: oil boom (Barroso II, 1922; "Venezuela Saudita" 1970s), immigrants, scholarships (1974), El Sistema (1975). Ends with Black Friday, Feb 18, 1983.
1999
Hugo Chávez takes office: nationalizations, price controls, close ties with Cuba.
2004 to 2009
The playbook: Supreme Court expanded (2004), RCTV license not renewed (2007), term limits removed (2009). Later: CLAP food tied to loyalty, colectivos, mass arrests (UN, HRW).
2013
Nicolás Maduro takes over. Oil output falls about 70% by 2023, hyperinflation, shortages.
2018
At El Junquito (Jan. 15), security forces killed Óscar Pérez, a police officer turned critic of Maduro, and six others. The government called it a shootout; Pérez had said in videos that he wanted to surrender, and the OAS human rights commission alleges extrajudicial executions. UN investigators later reported that Venezuela registered 5,287 killings in 2018 in security operations (many by the FAES special forces) and found reasonable grounds that many were extrajudicial executions (UN OHCHR, July 2019).
Nearly 8 million
Venezuelans have left the country (UN refugee agency). Many went to Colombia, Peru, Brazil, Chile and the U.S.
January 3, 2026
U.S. forces capture Maduro in Caracas. He faces federal charges in New York: narco-terrorism and cocaine-importation conspiracy (indicted 2020), and, in a superseding indictment unsealed Oct. 8, 2026, conspiracy to commit torture, including the torture of U.S. citizens. A UN fact-finding mission found crimes against humanity. He is in U.S. custody awaiting trial.
October 25, 2026
María Corina Machado (Nobel Peace Prize 2025) returns to Venezuela. Elections are expected in 2027; no date is set.
